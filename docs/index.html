Paperkites docs.

Paperkites is a place to put things down before they drift off: a thought on the train, a line from a meeting, the name of a book someone mentioned. It's a desktop app for notes and the small thoughts that come before them, and you drive it from the keyboard.

Most of what's worth keeping starts small, and most apps ask you to decide where it goes before you let yourself write it. Paperkites doesn't. You write first. The connections come later, from the #tags, [[links]] and @mentions you add as you go.

Paperkites is in development and not released yet. These pages describe the app as it is built today.

The ideas

Plain files you own. Every note is a plain markdown file in a folder on your disk. There's no database to export from and no account to sign in to. Git, grep, sync tools and other editors all work on your notes.

Keyboard first. Every action has a key. Paperkites borrows from vim: you move around in NORMAL mode, write in INSERT mode, and : opens a command line. If you'd rather not use vim keys, you can turn them off. The mouse works too.

Capture first. Paperkites opens on the capture box. Save a thought as a short fragment with ⌘↩, or turn it into a full note with ⌘E. Nothing you type there is thrown away.

A quiet screen. One column of text with a narrow gutter of details beside it. There's no toolbar, no tabs and no status bar. Messages appear in a small pill at the bottom of the window, and everything else is summoned from the keyboard.

Coming soon

coming soonSync. Sync built into Paperkites is on the way, with notes encrypted at rest and in sync, and the key staying with you. It isn't in the app yet.

Today your notes are plain files in a folder, so you can already sync them yourself with git or anything else that syncs a folder on your Mac (a vault in iCloud Drive or Dropbox is part of Premium). See Coming soon for how.

Help inside the app

:keys shows the full keymap from anywhere. It's built into the app, so it's there even if you edit or delete the welcome notes.
